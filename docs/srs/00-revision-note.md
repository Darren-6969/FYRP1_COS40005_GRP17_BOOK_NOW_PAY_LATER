# Revision Note

## V2.3 to V2.4

V2.3 added the architecture that V2.2 lacked. V2.4 closes the remaining gap between the specification and the plan it is meant to guide: the document described what to build but not how anyone would know it had worked, and its schedule was written against an assumed eight-week window rather than the real ten-week one.

| # | Area | Gap in V2.3 | Resolution in V2.4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Definition of success | The document had performance targets but no product measures. There was no statement of what the platform was meant to achieve, so at the end of the project the only available definition of success was that the features existed. | New Section 9: North Star metric, nine pilot exit criteria, the metric hierarchy, and the evaluation plan for the final report. |
| 2 | Measurement | Nothing in the system recorded whether the outcomes the requirements describe actually occurred. Measurement cannot be added retroactively, so a pilot run without it produces no evidence. | FR-ANL-002 product event instrumentation, the analytics_events table, FR-ADM-006 pilot metrics view, and a completeness test at 11.2.10. |
| 3 | Missing table | Section 6 built the entire asynchronous architecture and outbox behaviour on job_queue, but no such table existed in Section 5. This is the capability alignment failure that principle 5.2 exists to prevent, appearing in the document that states the principle. | job_queue added to 5.3.10 with lease and retry columns, matching the claiming mechanism in 6.3.2. |
| 4 | Operator onboarding | The document specified how a listing is created but never how an operator with an existing fleet of fifty vehicles gets started. Manual entry is hours of work and the largest single reason an operator abandons setup. | FR-LIST-006 bulk CSV import with per-row error reporting and idempotent re-upload. |
| 5 | Pilot readiness | The target is a pilot with real bookings, which puts published legal terms, live payment activation, operator identity verification, the tax invoicing position, a support rota and operator recruitment on the critical path. None of these were software tasks and none appeared anywhere. | Section 9.5 records all seven with owners, and they are tracked as epic E15 in the backlog. |
| 6 | Schedule and effort | The plan assumed eight weeks and 80 person-days. The real window is ten weeks, and decomposing the plan into 88 stories with acceptance criteria produced 92.5 person-days for the committed scope. | Section 10 rebuilt against the real calendar, 31 August to 8 November 2026. Required utilisation falls from about 67 percent to about 58 percent, every member sits inside capacity, and the heaviest single week drops from 6 days to 3.5. |

> The 12.5 person-day difference against the earlier estimate is not padding. It is work the module-level breakdown never listed: module scaffolding and the dependency lint rule, the job queue and worker, the tenant-aware database client, error tracking, product instrumentation and the pilot readiness workstream. Making it visible was the point.

> Sections 9 to 13 of V2.3 are renumbered to 10 to 14 to accommodate the new Section 9. The story-level backlog, per-member work plans and Gantt chart are maintained alongside this document and are the operational view of Section 10; where the two disagree, the backlog is correct because it is the one that gets updated.

## V2.2 to V2.3

V2.2 was a complete requirements and data specification, but it contained no architecture specification. Section 5 described what the system stores; nothing described how the code is organised, what may call what, what runs outside the request, or which architectural options were rejected and why. That gap is what this revision closes, through a new Section 6. Sections 6 to 12 of V2.2 are renumbered to 7 to 13.

| # | Area | Gap in V2.2 | Resolution in V2.3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Module boundaries | No component structure. Four developers were about to build six verticals into one Express application with no stated rule about what may call what, and no owner for any table. | Twelve modules across three tiers with every table assigned to exactly one owner, and a five-rule development covenant agreed in Phase 0 (6.2). |
| 2 | Dependency direction | Booking must ask Risk for the credit tier before a booking exists, while Risk needs booking outcomes and the tier determines the payment schedule owned by Payment. Any strict linear ordering of these three modules contains a cycle. | A Tier 0 orchestration layer owns cross-module transactions. Downward calls are policy queries; upward flow becomes asynchronous events, so Risk never reads the bookings table (6.2.3). |
| 3 | Asynchronous mechanism | The word asynchronously appeared in several requirements with no mechanism behind it, and outbound notifications had no delivery guarantee. In practice this becomes either a lost promise or a blocking call. | A PostgreSQL job queue with lease-based claiming, exponential backoff and dead-letter handling, doubling as the outbox because jobs are enqueued inside the business transaction (6.3.2, 6.3.3). |
| 4 | Security mechanisms | Row-level security depended on developers remembering to wrap every query. Authentication was one word. WhatsApp booking had no defined authentication at all, and matching on phone number alone would expose one customer’s bookings to whoever holds a recycled number. | A wrapped tenant-aware client with a lint rule and a system role for cross-tenant jobs; a full token lifecycle with immediate revocation; and verified identity linking with a restricted channel scope (6.4). |
| 5 | Resilience and contracts | No timeouts, no degradation behaviour, no API version prefix, and no shared error shape across three web surfaces and a mobile client. | A failure mode table covering every external dependency, a /v1 prefix with a deprecation policy, and a unified error contract with a request identifier threaded through logs (6.5, 6.6). |
| 6 | Trade-offs and evidence | Nothing recorded which architectural options were considered and rejected, so single-instance deployment and the absence of a message broker read as oversights rather than decisions. | Ten architecture decision records stating what was chosen, what was given up and what would trigger revisiting it, plus architecture conformance and job reliability tests (6.8, 10.2.8, 10.2.9). |

> Two corrections carried in from the review of the proposed architecture. A separate outbox table alongside the job queue was rejected as duplicate machinery, since both are durable rows written in a business transaction and drained by a worker. And the claim that role-based code splitting prevents information disclosure is not correct: an unloaded chunk is still served from the same origin, so it is recorded in 6.7.1 as a bundle size measure only.

## V2.1 to V2.2

V2.1 corrected the data architecture issues raised against V2.0. V2.2 addressed the remaining blocking problems, which fell into six groups.

| # | Area | Defect in V2.1 | Resolution in V2.2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Data model | The bookings table had no service date columns. The dates being booked were held inside booking_details JSONB, so no-show marking, balance due dates, daily inventory decrement and settlement triggers all depended on a value that could not be queried or indexed. | Added service_start_at, service_end_at and quantity as first-class columns with supporting indexes (Section 5.3.5). |
| 2 | Inventory | FR-LIST-003 specified a single-row atomic decrement, and the concurrency test in 9.2.1 tested a single row. A multi-day rental spans several rows, so multi-day oversell was still possible and the test would have passed anyway. | Multi-day range decrement defined as one statement with a row-count assertion (Section 4.1.3), plus a second concurrency test for overlapping multi-day bookings (Section 11.2.2). |
| 3 | Credit control | No-show auto-completed the booking, which made it settleable revenue even with an unpaid balance. The deposit-paid-then-absent case was unclassified. The appeal path promised in 3.6.4 had no data model. The tier engine was purely reactive, so a new account could hold many bookings before any rule triggered. | no_show and no_show_unpaid are terminal statuses with a full outcome matrix (Section 3.5.4). Appeal state folded into customer_risk_events. New concurrent exposure cap (FR-RISK-005). |
| 4 | Finance | The commission arithmetic was never written down, and platform-funded promotions could silently produce negative platform margin. The payment processing fee was described in prose but had no ledger column. bookings.promotion_id duplicated promo_redemptions. | Both commission formulas stated with worked examples and a discount cap rule (Section 3.1.5). Added stripe_fee_amount and funded_by to commission_ledger. Removed bookings.promotion_id. |
| 5 | Integrity and correctness | documents used a polymorphic owner that cannot carry a foreign key. user_platform_roles allowed only one platform role per user, contradicting the multi-identity goal. payments referenced both a schedule and a booking with nothing enforcing agreement. Row-level security was asserted without a mechanism. No timezone policy existed. | Documents de-polymorphised with a CHECK constraint. Composite key and composite foreign keys added. RLS session mechanism specified (FR-SEC-002). Timezone policy added as NFR-DATA-001. |
| 6 | Plan and academic rigour | Phase 0-3 was stated as roughly 70 person-days but the work breakdown summed to 78 against 72 available. Member A carried about 42 of those days while Members B and C had no work until week 4. The traceability matrix referenced six requirement IDs that did not exist in the document body, and two that pointed at the wrong requirement. | Work reallocated across four members with an API contract task added in Phase 0. Capacity arithmetic restated honestly with a week-4 checkpoint and pre-agreed contingency (Section 10). Requirement IDs renumbered by module and the matrix rebuilt from the body text (Appendix A). |

> Two internal contradictions in V2.1 are also corrected: Section 10.1 placed Phase 3 in week 6 while the Gantt chart ran user acceptance testing and deployment into week 7, and the Gantt started the credit risk module in week 3 while the order module it depends on ran into week 4. Both are resolved in Section 10.4.
